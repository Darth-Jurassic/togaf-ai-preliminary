# Product Development Process Definition

## Context

[Product Context](Product%20Context.md) requires each adopting project to define
its own purpose and boundaries. Architecture work should support learning from
product feedback, practitioner signals, adoption evidence, delivery friction,
engineering constraints and decision-control concerns, not only document a target
software state.

The core preliminary question is therefore not only which architecture framework
to use first. The core question is what kind of product development process
capability the adopting project needs.

Architecture work should behave as part of a lean software product
development process: make evidence visible, preserve original signals, reduce
waste, expose trade-offs, validate learning with stakeholders, decide where
human judgment is required, automate routine work where AI is reliable, and
convert validated learning into product goals, constraints, options, decisions,
and improvement work.

## Decision

We will align architecture work with lean software product development and use
architecture governance as a control surface for the development process.

Architecture work must support an explicit learning loop from product and
technology feedback into product learning, product decisions, delivery
improvements, and technology improvement work. Architecture artifacts are not
valuable only because they are complete; they are valuable when they improve
product learning, reduce avoidable loss, and help the organization make better
product and technology decisions.

The development process must be flexible about control. The harness resolves
routine, deterministic matters from authoritative context and proceeds without
creating artificial approval work or recording internal reasoning. A person
enters the loop only when required information is unavailable, materially
different valid choices require human intent, or policy, exception, or risk
acceptance requires human authority. AI may automate discovery, traceability,
consistency checks, drafting, gap detection, synchronization, and other
high-volume work while the process retains durable outcomes and evidence.

This preliminary record is the senior process decision. Later preliminary
decisions select methods, workflow, notation, report structure, and scope
because they satisfy the process requirements defined here.

## Process Requirements

The product development process must satisfy these requirements:

- Preserve raw feedback and source context before interpretation.
- Trace feedback, ideas, risks, and problems into stakeholders, categories,
  learning records, product goals, constraints, options, and decisions.
- Provide clear ways to streamline product-related information into learning
  loop inputs.
- Convert validated learning into product goals, measurable outcomes,
  constraints, options, decisions, experiments, and improvement work.
- Make the lean loss of ignored signals explicit enough to compare reports and
  explain priority.
- Allow goals, constraints, options, and decisions to be revisited when product
  learning changes the evidence base.
- Involve accountable stakeholder roles in ingest validation and in product or
  technology decisions that genuinely require human intent or authority.
- Define where human input, exception decisions, or risk acceptance is mandatory;
  do not impose blanket phase approval after the complete gate passes.
- Let AI-assisted automation resolve, draft, check, synchronize, classify, and
  apply deterministic changes within its authority, escalating only unresolved
  human blockers.
- Keep architecture artifacts lightweight, versionable, reviewable, and suitable
  for iteration.
- Support decision control by checking evidence, traceability, decision quality,
  and learning status rather than document completeness alone.

## Consequences

- Process selection must be judged by how well each candidate process
  supports the lean software product learning loop and decision-control
  requirements above.
- Method, notation, and tooling choices are subordinate decisions, not the core
  premise.
- Product and technology feedback must stay connected to product learning
  because current evidence is the basis for future product and technology
  decisions.
- Preliminary decisions should be ordered from core operating philosophy to
  consequence decisions and implementation rules.
